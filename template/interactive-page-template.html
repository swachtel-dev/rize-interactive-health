What the derivative actually measures

Before the rules, the idea: a derivative is a slope you sneak up on.

Pick two points on a curve and draw the straight line through them. That line is a secant, and its slope tells you the average rate of change between the two points. Nothing mysterious: rise over run, the same slope you have computed since algebra.

Now slide the second point toward the first. The secant pivots. The average rate of change it reports is measured over a shorter and shorter interval, so it describes the curve closer and closer to that one spot. The derivative is the number that slope is heading toward.

Why "limit" and not just "plug in zero"

Setting h to zero directly gives you 0/0, which is not a number. The limit is the tool that lets you ask what the quotient approaches without ever evaluating it at the forbidden point.
